Ednovate Labs · The Gambia and ECOWAS 1

The whole path

Follow Awa, from a question to a job.

Awa Sanneh lives in Kanifing. She wants work that uses numbers, and she does not know which course to start. Every screen in the demo is a step on this line.

  1. 1

    Career Compass asks five questions

    What she enjoys, where she is starting, how she learns best, how much time she really has, and what would make it worth it.

  2. 2

    She gets a course, and she can say no

    The suggestion is Data Analytics Foundations, onsite at the Kanifing lab. Awa chooses. The guide does not enrol her.

  3. 3

    She learns by doing a real task

    A module ends when she can do the thing — clean a messy list, write a query, explain a chart — not when the weeks run out.

  4. 4

    Her tutor reads the work and signs the skill

    Fatoumatta Jallow checks what Awa submits. She can sign it, or send it back with a note. A computer never signs a skill.

  5. 5

    An employer sees the signed skills, and the reason

    A match says why it was made, and which skill is still missing. Work that is still with the tutor stays hidden.

One record, not a new certificate every course. The Skills Passport keeps growing. A later course adds to it. It does not wipe the page clean.

Step 2 · Learning Studio

The same course, three ways to take it.

Awa picks the way that fits her week. She can switch later. The skills at the end are the same.

Onsite

In a lab, with a tutor and classmates. Kanifing, Banjul, Serrekunda and Janjanbureh.

Live, with a tutor

Classes at a set time, joined from home. A person is teaching. It is not a recording.

At her own pace

She moves through it in the hours she actually has. Around a job, or around school.

Finished means she can do it.

Each module ends with a piece of work that goes into the passport. Sitting through the class is not enough, and it is not the thing an employer is shown.

The moment that matters

A person signs the skill. The screen does not.

Awa writes what she did and what she decided, and attaches the file. Then it leaves her hands.

  1. 1

    Awa sends the work

    A title a reviewer would recognise, a few sentences on what she found, and the file itself.

  2. 2

    It waits in Fatoumatta’s queue

    The tutor sees the task, the file, and a draft note she is free to rewrite. The draft is a starting point, not the decision.

She signs it

The skill turns green on the passport. Awa’s name, the tutor’s name, and the date stay on it. Employers can now see that line.

or

She sends it back

Awa gets a note on what to fix. The skill stays off the public record until the work is signed. Nothing is quietly approved.

Within two working days. That is the promise on the submission screen. The queue on the tutor’s home page shows how many pieces of work are still waiting.

The tutor’s morning

Three screens. Then the picture feeds back.

Fatoumatta does not start from a spreadsheet she maintains herself. The morning is the same three stops, in this order.

01

The roster

Every learner, the course, how far they are, and a plain flag: on track, worth a conversation, or at risk.

→
02

The queue

Work waiting to be read. She checks it against the task, rewrites the note if she wants, then signs or returns it.

→
03

The region

Signed skills set against open roles, country by country across ECOWAS. No learner’s name is on this page.

The loop closes

What the region is short of becomes the next course to open. The passport is not the end of the story.
